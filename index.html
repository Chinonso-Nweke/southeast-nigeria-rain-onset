Best planting date

This combines two things: the dry spell risk you saw above and the cost of waiting. Planting later is safer from dry spells but loses part of the season. Say how costly waiting is, and the tool picks the date with the lowest total loss. It uses the dry spell length chosen above.

Because the rainfall sources disagree, it also finds a robust date: the one whose worst case across all sources and periods is smallest.

How it works: for each source and period (all years, and the last 20), loss = (share of years with a dry spell of the chosen length) + (cost of waiting per week x weeks after 1 March). The dry spell curve is averaged over seven dates so that one odd year cannot choose the date. The robust date minimises the worst regret: how much more loss it gives than the best date for whichever source and period turns out to be right. This is a simple model of rainfall risk and waiting cost only. It does not know your crop, soil or seed, so treat it as a way to see the trade-off, not as planting advice.

    Agreement is rated by a rule of thumb of my own, not an official standard: weak if yearly totals correlate below 0.5, or the later-start estimates differ by more than 10 days, or the change in yearly rain differs by more than 8 percentage points; strong only if all of these are much tighter. Neither source is a rain gauge measurement, so agreement does not prove either is right.

    The rule used, its limits and the data checks

    Working rule for the start of the rains:

    • The rainfall figures are satellite and model estimates (NASA POWER on a grid of roughly 50 km, ERA5 of roughly 25 km), not rain gauge readings from a farm.
    • The rule is adapted from Sivakumar (1988), who studied drier parts of West Africa. We apply it to a wetter zone and start looking on 1 March, so the numbers are a working choice, not an official standard for south-east Nigeria.
    • A false start means an early wet spell was followed by a dry spell of 7 days or more. The percentage depends on the rule, so always read it together with the rule.
    • Past patterns do not guarantee future seasons. Nothing here is planting advice for a particular farm.
