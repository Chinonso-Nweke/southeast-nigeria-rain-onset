When do the rains start in south-east Nigeria?

Planting too early risks losing seedlings to a dry spell. Planting too late wastes part of the season. This page uses 44 years of daily rainfall to show when the rains have usually started and how often early rains were followed by a dry spell.

Check a planting date

Share of years (1981 to 2024) with a dry spell of the chosen length in the 30 days after each planting date. A dry day is a day under 1 mm of rain. This looks at rainfall only. It ignores soil, crop and seed type, so it is not advice for a particular farm.

The rule used and its limits

Working rule for the start of the rains:

  • The rainfall figures are satellite and model estimates for a grid area of roughly 50 km, not rain gauge readings from a farm.
  • The rule is adapted from Sivakumar (1988), who studied drier parts of West Africa. We apply it to a wetter zone and start looking on 1 March, so the numbers are a working choice, not an official standard for south-east Nigeria.
  • A false start means an early wet spell was followed by a dry spell of 7 days or more. The percentage depends on the rule, so always read it together with the rule.
  • The later start is a pattern in this one dataset. It still needs checking against a second rainfall source.
  • Past patterns do not guarantee future seasons. Nothing here is planting advice for a particular farm.
