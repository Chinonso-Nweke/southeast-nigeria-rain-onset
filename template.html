Best planting date

This combines two things: the dry spell risk you saw above and the cost of waiting. Planting later is safer from dry spells but loses part of the season. Say how costly waiting is, and the tool picks the date with the lowest total loss. It uses the dry spell length chosen above.

How it works: loss = (share of years with a dry spell of the chosen length) + (cost of waiting per week x weeks after 1 March). The dry spell curve is averaged over seven dates so that one odd year cannot choose the date. This is a simple model of rainfall risk and waiting cost only. It does not know your crop, soil or seed, so treat it as a way to see the trade-off, not as planting advice.

The rule used, its limits and the data checks

Working rule for the start of the rains:

  • The rainfall figures are satellite and model estimates for a grid area of roughly 50 km, not rain gauge readings from a farm.
  • The rule is adapted from Sivakumar (1988), who studied drier parts of West Africa. We apply it to a wetter zone and start looking on 1 March, so the numbers are a working choice, not an official standard for south-east Nigeria.
  • A false start means an early wet spell was followed by a dry spell of 7 days or more. The percentage depends on the rule, so always read it together with the rule.
  • The later start and the matching fall in yearly rain are patterns in NASA POWER. A comparison with ERA5, a second rainfall product, showed a smaller later start (3 to 13 days, not 19 to 30) and almost no fall in yearly rain, and the two products agreed only weakly. Treat the later start as unconfirmed, and read the risk numbers as indicative.
  • Past patterns do not guarantee future seasons. Nothing here is planting advice for a particular farm.
